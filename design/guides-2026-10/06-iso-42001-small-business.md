# ISO 42001 for small businesses: do you actually need it?

**Last reviewed: 16 October 2026.** Practical guidance, not legal advice.

## The short answer

Probably not yet. ISO/IEC 42001 is a voluntary international standard for managing AI responsibly. No UK or EU law requires a small business to be certified. Most small firms are better served by a few proportionate basics (a policy, a list of the AI tools in use, a training record and a clear note of where a person checks the output) and should consider certification only if a major customer asks for it.

## What ISO 42001 is

ISO/IEC 42001 was published in December 2023. It's the first international standard for an **AI management system**: the policies, roles, risk assessments and checks an organisation uses to develop or use AI responsibly.

If you know ISO 27001 (information security) or ISO 9001 (quality), it will feel familiar. It uses the same high-level structure, with requirements covering:

- the context of your organisation and how you use AI
- leadership and responsibilities
- planning, including AI risk and impact assessments
- support, such as skills, awareness and documentation
- running and controlling your AI systems
- monitoring, internal audit and management review
- continual improvement

An annex sets out controls grouped into nine areas, including AI policies, roles, impact assessment, the AI system lifecycle, data, information for users, how AI is used, and suppliers. You don't have to adopt every control; you record which apply and why in a "statement of applicability".

## ISO 42001 is not the law

The EU AI Act is law. ISO 42001 is a voluntary standard. Being certified doesn't on its own make you compliant with the EU AI Act, and you can comply with the Act without being certified. The records you'd build for ISO 42001, such as risk assessments and a register of AI systems, can help show you've taken the Act seriously.

If you're not sure whether the EU rules reach you at all, start with [Does the EU AI Act apply to your UK business?](/guides/eu-ai-act-uk/)

## Who asks for it

Requests usually come from:

- **large customers** adding AI questions to supplier questionnaires and due diligence
- **public-sector buyers** in some tenders
- **businesses that build AI products** for others, where certification is part of selling to bigger organisations

If none of your customers has asked, that's a good sign you don't need it yet.

## What certification involves

1. **Gap assessment.** Compare what you do today with the standard.
2. **Build the management system.** Policies, roles, risk and impact assessments, the statement of applicability, and records.
3. **Run it for a while** so there's evidence that it works in practice.
4. **Stage 1 audit.** An accredited certification body reviews your documents.
5. **Stage 2 audit.** They check the controls actually operate.
6. **Keep it up.** Certificates typically last three years, with an audit each year to keep them valid.

Industry estimates put the whole path at roughly 6 to 12 months, and less if you already hold ISO 27001. Costs depend on your size, how much AI you use and the certification body, so get quotes before committing.

## ISO 42001 vs ISO 27001

| | ISO 27001 | ISO 42001 |
|---|---|---|
| Covers | Information security | Responsible development and use of AI |
| Typical request | Customers checking you protect their data | Customers checking you use AI responsibly |
| Structure | Management system plus controls | The same high-level structure, plus AI-specific controls |
| Together | Can share policies, audits and reviews with ISO 42001 | Usually quicker to add if you already have ISO 27001 |

## The proportionate alternative

For a business of 5 to 50 people, these cover most of what customers ask about, and most of the spirit of ISO 42001:

| What customers ask | What answers it |
|---|---|
| What AI do you use? | A list of your AI tools, what they're used for and what data goes in. Our [free AI policy template](/resources/ai-policy-template/) includes one. |
| What are the rules? | A one-page AI policy covering approved tools, data and checking. |
| Are your staff trained? | A short session and a record of who attended. See [AI literacy training for staff](/guides/ai-literacy-training-staff/). |
| Who checks the output? | A clear note of where a person signs off, especially on anything sent to customers or decisions about people. |
| What happens if it goes wrong? | A simple incident step: who to tell, how fast, and when to report a data breach. |

If you later go for certification, none of this is wasted. It becomes the start of your management system.

## Be careful with AI "badges"

Genuine ISO 42001 certification is issued by certification bodies that are themselves accredited; in the UK, accreditation is by UKAS. You'll also see private "AI trust" schemes and badges. Some may be useful, but before paying for one, check who runs it, what's actually assessed, whether anyone independent checks it, and whether your customers recognise it.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is ISO 42001?**
ISO/IEC 42001 is the international standard for an AI management system, published in December 2023. It sets out how an organisation should govern its development or use of AI, including policies, roles, risk assessments and monitoring.

**Is ISO 42001 mandatory?**
No. It's voluntary. No UK or EU law requires a small business to be certified, though some large customers and public-sector buyers may ask for it.

**How long does ISO 42001 certification take?**
Industry estimates suggest roughly 6 to 12 months from start to certificate, and less if you already hold ISO 27001. After that, there's an audit each year and recertification every three years.

**What's the difference between ISO 42001 and ISO 27001?**
ISO 27001 covers information security. ISO 42001 covers the responsible development and use of AI. They share the same structure, so firms with ISO 27001 can usually add ISO 42001 more quickly.

**Does ISO 42001 make me compliant with the EU AI Act?**
Not on its own. The EU AI Act is law and ISO 42001 is a voluntary standard. The records you build for ISO 42001 can help, but you still need to meet the Act's specific obligations if it applies to you.
