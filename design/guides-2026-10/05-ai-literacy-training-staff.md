# AI literacy training for staff: what small businesses need to do

**Last reviewed: 12 October 2026.** Practical guidance, not legal advice.

## The short answer

If the EU AI Act applies to your business, Article 4 asks you to take measures to support AI literacy among the people who use AI on your behalf. Since 27 July 2026 that's an obligation of effort: no exam, no certificate, no guaranteed standard. A short, relevant training session and a record that it happened is a sensible way to meet it.

Even if the EU rules don't reach you, it's worth doing. Microsoft and Censuswide found 71% of UK workers have used AI tools their employer never approved. Training is how you turn that from a risk into a benefit.

Not sure if the EU rules apply? Check [Does the EU AI Act apply to your UK business?](/guides/eu-ai-act-uk/)

## What Article 4 says now

The original rule, in force since 2 February 2025, asked businesses to ensure a sufficient level of AI literacy. The July 2026 changes softened that. Businesses that build or use AI now have to take measures to support AI literacy among their staff and others using AI for them, taking account of:

- what those people already know, their experience and training
- the context the AI is used in
- the people the AI is used on or affects

The amended text says plainly that you don't have to guarantee any particular level of literacy for any individual.

## What "AI literacy" means for a small team

It doesn't mean understanding how AI models work. For a business of 5 to 50 people, it means everyone who uses AI at work knows:

1. **Which tools they're allowed to use**, and why personal accounts are off limits.
2. **What never goes into an AI tool**: personal data, confidential client information, passwords.
3. **That AI gets things wrong**, confidently, and how to check its work.
4. **That they're responsible** for anything they send, whether or not AI drafted it.
5. **When to tell people AI was involved**, such as chatbots and realistic AI images.
6. **Who to ask**, and what to do if something goes wrong.

## A training plan you can run in a week

**Day 1: find out what's in use (30 minutes).**
Ask everyone, with no blame attached, which AI tools they use for work and what for. You'll learn where the time savings are as well as where the risks are.

**Day 2: write or update your policy (1 hour).**
Use our [free AI policy template](/resources/ai-policy-template/). Approve the tools people actually find useful, on business accounts.

**Day 3: run a team session (60 to 90 minutes).**
Go through the policy together, then spend most of the time hands-on:

- Ask an approved tool a question you know the answer to and see where it goes wrong.
- Practise checking an AI-drafted email or quote against the source.
- Walk through what to do if customer data goes into the wrong tool.

**Day 4: role-specific top-ups (15 to 30 minutes each).**
Whoever runs the chatbot, does the accounts or handles hiring gets a short session on the risks in their area. Hiring and anything else that involves decisions about people needs the most care.

**Day 5: write it down (10 minutes).**
Record the session (see below) and set a date to revisit it.

Repeat the session when someone joins or when you add a significant new AI tool.

## What to record

You don't need a certificate. Keep a simple record:

| Date | Who attended | What was covered | Tools covered | Run by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Date] | [Names] | [Policy walk-through, checking output, data rules] | [Tools] | [Name] |

Store it with your AI policy. If anyone ever asks what you've done, that page is the answer.

## Do you need an AI literacy course?

For most small businesses, a generic online course is less useful than an hour spent on the tools your team actually uses. Courses are fine as background, but they don't know your data, your customers or your policy. If you do use one, add a session on your own tools and rules.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is AI literacy training mandatory?**
If the EU AI Act applies to you, you must take measures to support AI literacy, and training is the obvious measure. Since July 2026 the rule doesn't require any specific level or certificate.

**Who needs AI literacy training?**
Anyone who uses AI tools on the business's behalf, including contractors and temporary staff.

**How long should AI literacy training take?**
For a small team, 60 to 90 minutes of hands-on training plus short role-specific top-ups is a sensible start. Repeat it when tools or people change.

**Do I need an AI literacy certificate?**
No. Keep your own record of who was trained, when, and on what.
