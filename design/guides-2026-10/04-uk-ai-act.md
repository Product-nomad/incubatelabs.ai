# Is there a UK AI Act?

**Last reviewed: 12 October 2026.** Practical guidance, not legal advice.

## The short answer

No. The UK has no AI Act, and the King's Speech in May 2026 didn't announce one. Instead, the UK regulates AI through the laws that already exist, enforced by the regulators you already know: the ICO for data, the CMA for consumers and competition, and sector regulators like the FCA.

That doesn't mean there are no rules. If your business uses AI with customer or staff data, these are the ones that matter.

## 1. UK GDPR still applies to everything

Any AI tool that touches personal data is covered by UK GDPR, the same as any other software. The basics:

- Know what personal data goes into which tool, and why.
- Use tools whose providers give you proper data protection terms. Business accounts usually do; personal accounts usually don't.
- Tell people in your privacy notice if you use AI with their data in ways they wouldn't expect.
- Report serious personal data breaches to the ICO within 72 hours. That includes customer data pasted into the wrong tool.

## 2. New rules on automated decisions (since 5 February 2026)

The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 replaced the old UK GDPR rule on automated decision-making. It came into force on 5 February 2026.

A decision is "solely automated" if no person meaningfully takes part in it. If such a decision has a legal or similarly significant effect on someone, like refusing credit, rejecting a job application or ending a contract, you can now make it on more lawful bases than before, but only with safeguards in place. You must:

- tell the person about the decision
- let them make representations
- offer genuine human intervention
- let them contest the decision

The ICO's draft guidance says the person reviewing must have the authority, competence and discretion to change the outcome. Rubber-stamping doesn't count.

Stricter limits still apply to decisions based on special category data, such as health or ethnicity.

**For a small business, the simplest approach is the one we recommend anyway:** let AI prepare information and suggest, and have a person make every decision about people.

## 3. Regulators are writing AI-specific guidance

- **ICO:** consulted on draft guidance on automated decision-making and profiling in spring 2026, and is required by regulations made in May 2026 to prepare a statutory code of practice on AI.
- **Other regulators** apply their existing rules to AI in their sectors. If you're FCA-regulated, for example, your AI use is covered by the same conduct rules as everything else you do.

## 4. Other law still applies to what AI does

- **Equality Act 2010:** if an AI tool helps screen candidates or set terms for customers and it disadvantages people with protected characteristics, you're responsible, not the tool.
- **Consumer law:** what your chatbot tells customers about prices, refunds or terms can bind you. In 2024 a Canadian tribunal made Air Canada honour the wrong bereavement-fare advice its chatbot gave a customer. UK courts haven't ruled on a case like it yet, but don't let your chatbot make promises you won't keep.
- **Contracts and confidentiality:** client contracts often limit where their information can go. Check before client documents go into AI tools.

## 5. The EU AI Act may apply anyway

No UK AI Act doesn't mean no AI Act. If your AI tools are used in the EU, or their output is, parts of the EU AI Act probably apply to you. See [Does the EU AI Act apply to your UK business?](/guides/eu-ai-act-uk/)

## What to do

1. **List your AI tools** and what data goes into each.
2. **Move work onto business accounts** with proper data terms.
3. **Update your privacy notice** if you use AI with customer data in ways people wouldn't expect.
4. **Keep a person in charge of every decision about people.**
5. **Write it down** in a short AI policy. Start with our [free AI policy template](/resources/ai-policy-template/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there a UK AI Act?**
No. The UK has no AI Act, and none was announced in the May 2026 King's Speech. AI is covered by existing law, such as UK GDPR, and by regulators' guidance.

**Is AI regulated in the UK?**
Yes, through existing law. UK GDPR, the new automated decision rules, the Equality Act and consumer law all apply to how you use AI.

**Can AI make decisions about customers or staff in the UK?**
Fully automated decisions with significant effects are allowed only with safeguards: telling the person, letting them respond, offering human review and letting them challenge it. The simplest route is a person making the final call.

**Does the EU AI Act apply in the UK?**
It applies to UK businesses whose AI is used in the EU or whose AI output is used there.
