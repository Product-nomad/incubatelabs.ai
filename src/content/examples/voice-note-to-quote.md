# From a voice note on site to a quote ready to send

**Worked example.** The business and figures below are illustrative, based on a typical firm of this size. We measure yours in the first conversation.

## The business

An 8-person electrical contractor. The owner prices most jobs, usually in the evening after a day on the tools. About 15 quotes go out a week. The slow ones lose work to whoever replied first.

## How it works today

1. The owner looks at the job and scribbles notes, or tries to remember.
2. That evening, they type the quote up line by line, looking up rates in last month's quotes.
3. Around 25 minutes per quote, often days after the visit.
4. Nobody follows up. Quotes that go quiet stay quiet.

## What we'd build

1. **On site, the owner records a voice note** describing the job: "Kitchen rewire at Mill Lane, six double sockets, two downlight circuits, new consumer unit, certification."
2. **The note is transcribed and matched to their own price list.** Each item becomes a quote line with the right rate code and quantity. Nothing is priced from thin air: if an item isn't on the list, it's flagged.
3. **A draft quote lands in their inbox** in their usual template, with anything uncertain highlighted, such as "downlight type not mentioned".
4. **They check it, adjust it and send it.** Two to five minutes instead of twenty-five.
5. **If the customer hasn't replied after five days**, a polite follow-up is drafted for the owner to approve.

## Where a person checks

- Every quote is approved by the owner before it goes anywhere.
- Anything not on the price list, or anything the voice note left unclear, is flagged rather than guessed.
- Follow-ups are drafted, never sent automatically.

## Time back

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Time per quote | about 25 minutes | about 5 minutes to check and send |
| Quotes a week | 15 | 15 |
| Time a week | about 6 hours 15 minutes | about 1 hour 15 minutes |

**About 5 hours a week back**, and quotes go out the same day. Our working: 15 quotes × 20 minutes saved = 300 minutes.

## What it runs on

The phone they already carry, the price list they already keep, and their existing email and accounts software. The AI part is small: transcribing the note and matching items to the price list. The rest is ordinary automation.

## What it doesn't do

It doesn't set prices, decide margins or send anything on its own. The owner's price list and judgement stay in charge.
