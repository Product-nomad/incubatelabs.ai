# Does the EU AI Act apply to your UK business?

**Last reviewed: 16 October 2026.** Practical guidance, not legal advice.

## The short answer

Brexit didn't take UK businesses out of the EU AI Act. It applies when your AI reaches the EU, not because of where you're based. If you use AI with customers, clients or people in the EU, or the work your AI produces is used there, some of it probably applies to you.

The good news: for most small businesses that's two things, not two hundred. The heavy rules people worry about apply to a narrow set of uses, and they've been pushed back to December 2027.

## Three questions to find out where you stand

**1. Does your AI reach the EU?**
Do you have customers, clients, website visitors or suppliers in the EU who see or use something your AI tools produce? A chatbot on a website that EU visitors use counts. So does a report drafted with AI for a client in Dublin.

- **No:** the EU AI Act very likely doesn't apply. The UK rules still do: see [Is there a UK AI Act?](/guides/uk-ai-act/)
- **Yes:** carry on.

**2. Do you use AI that talks to people or creates content?**
A chatbot, an AI phone or email assistant, AI-generated images, video or audio that could pass as real.

- **Yes:** the transparency rules apply to you now. See [Article 50 and chatbots](/guides/eu-ai-act-article-50-chatbots/).

**3. Does AI make or shape decisions about people?**
Screening CVs, deciding who gets credit, scoring staff performance, setting prices for individuals.

- **Yes:** these are "high-risk" uses. The full rules start on 2 December 2027, but the UK's own rules on automated decisions already apply. Get advice before you build or buy.

Whatever your answers, one rule applies to every business in scope: **AI literacy**. That's covered below.

## Who the Act calls what

- **Provider:** whoever builds an AI system, or has one built and puts it into use under their own name.
- **Deployer:** any business using an AI system at work. If your team uses ChatGPT, Copilot or an AI tool in your accounts software, you're a deployer.

Most small businesses are deployers. Deployers have far fewer duties than providers. If you've had a custom chatbot or tool built and run it under your own brand, you may count as its provider, so check that case properly.

## What applies now

### AI literacy (since 2 February 2025, softened July 2026)

If you're in scope, you must take measures to support AI literacy among the people who use AI on your behalf. Changes in force since 27 July 2026 made this an obligation of effort: you don't have to guarantee any particular level of knowledge, and no certificate is needed.

In practice: a short policy, a training session matched to the tools your team uses, and a record that it happened. See [AI literacy training for staff](/guides/ai-literacy-training-staff/).

### Transparency (since 2 August 2026)

- Chatbots and AI assistants must make clear people are dealing with AI, unless that's obvious.
- Deepfakes, meaning AI images, audio or video that look like real people, places or events, must be labelled.
- AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest must be disclosed, unless a person has genuinely reviewed it and takes editorial responsibility.
- Tools that generate content must mark it in a machine-readable way. That's mostly the tool maker's job. Tools already on the market before August 2026 have until 2 December 2026.

### Banned practices (since 2 February 2025)

A short list of uses is banned outright. The ones a small business could stumble into:

- Using AI to read staff emotions from their faces or voices at work, for example scoring how employees sound on calls. (Medical and safety uses are exempt.)
- AI designed to manipulate people in ways that cause them harm, or to exploit people's age, disability or financial situation.
- "Social scoring" of people based on their behaviour or personal traits.

From 2 December 2026, AI tools that create non-consensual intimate images or child sexual abuse material are banned as well.

## What has been delayed

The July 2026 changes moved the high-risk rules back:

| Date | What starts |
|---|---|
| 2 February 2025 | Banned practices; AI literacy |
| 2 August 2025 | Rules for the makers of general-purpose AI models (not you) |
| 27 July 2026 | Softer AI literacy wording |
| 2 August 2026 | Transparency rules for chatbots, deepfakes and AI-generated text |
| 2 December 2026 | Machine-readable marking for older generative tools; new bans |
| 2 December 2027 | High-risk rules for uses like recruitment, credit and education |
| 2 August 2028 | High-risk rules for AI built into regulated products |

## What the July 2026 changes mean for small firms

- More time before the high-risk rules: December 2027 instead of August 2026.
- A softer AI literacy duty: effort, not a guaranteed result.
- Simpler paperwork for SMEs that build high-risk systems, and lower fine caps.
- Nothing changed for chatbots and deepfakes. Those rules started on time.

## What about fines?

Breaking the transparency rules can mean fines of up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide turnover. For SMEs, the lower of the two amounts applies. Regulators are expected to start with the obvious cases, but "nobody's been fined yet" isn't a plan.

## What to do this month

1. **List the AI tools your team actually uses**, including personal ChatGPT accounts. Most owners find more than they expected.
2. **Check where they reach.** Any EU customers, clients or website visitors?
3. **Make your chatbot say it's AI** in the first message, if you have one.
4. **Write a one-page AI policy.** Start with our [free AI policy template](/resources/ai-policy-template/).
5. **Run a short training session** and keep a record of who attended and what was covered.
6. **Put a person in charge of any decision about people.** No AI-only decisions on hiring, pay or credit.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does the EU AI Act apply after Brexit?**
Yes, if your AI reaches the EU. It applies to businesses outside the EU when their AI systems are used in the EU or the output is used there.

**I only use ChatGPT. Does it apply to me?**
If you use it at work and the results reach people in the EU, you're a deployer. Your main duty is AI literacy, and transparency if you publish AI content or run an AI chatbot.

**When does the EU AI Act come into force?**
It's already in force and applying in stages. Banned practices and AI literacy have applied since February 2025, transparency since August 2026, and the high-risk rules start in December 2027.

**Do I need an AI literacy certificate?**
No. Since July 2026 the rule is to take measures to support AI literacy. Keep your own record of training.

**Is there a UK version of the AI Act?**
No. The UK has no AI Act, but UK GDPR and new rules on automated decisions still apply. See [Is there a UK AI Act?](/guides/uk-ai-act/)

**Do I need an AI certification, such as ISO/IEC 42001?**
No law requires a small business to hold one. [ISO/IEC 42001](/guides/iso-42001-small-business/) is the international standard for running an AI management system, and some larger customers may ask about it or about other schemes. For most small firms, a written AI policy, a list of the AI tools you use, a training record and a note of where a person checks the output answer most of the questions customers ask. If a major customer insists on certification, that's the point to look at it.
